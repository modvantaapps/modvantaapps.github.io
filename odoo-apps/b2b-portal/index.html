B2B Wholesale Portal

Turn your Odoo shop into a purchasing portal for trade customers

View on Odoo Apps Store Ask about customisation

What it does

Wholesale buyers know their codes, buy in cases and reorder the same lines every month. Give them a B2B account page, quick order by SKU, agreed contract prices, reorder suggestions, order templates, a trade credit page and a catalogue of their own.

Odoo's shop is built for a consumer buying one item. A trade buyer needs to type forty product codes, order in full cases, see their agreed prices and their credit, and repeat last month's order. This module adds that to the customer portal and the shop.

Products get a minimum quantity, an increment and a case size; quick-order quantities that break them are raised to the nearest allowed quantity and the buyer is told why. Customer agreements hold negotiated prices and committed quantities, and the agreed price is charged on the buyer's cart while the agreement is in force.

In the backend you get B2B customer and product summaries, customer agreements, catalogues per customer or group, order templates, approval rules and credit limit requests to review. There is no public sign-up form: staff enter wholesale applications under Website > Configuration > B2B > Applications, and approving one creates the customer and its portal login.

Who it is for

  • Wholesalers whose buyers order by product code. A trade buyer with a printed order sheet doesn't want to search the shop forty times. The quick order page takes pasted codes and quantities or an uploaded CSV or Excel file, and reports line by line what was added, rounded to the case size, or rejected.
  • Distributors with exclusive or regional ranges. Some customers shouldn't see some products at all. A catalogue assigns products or whole categories to a customer, a customer group or every B2B customer, and anything outside it disappears from that buyer's shop and quick order pad.
  • Manufacturers selling to dealers on negotiated terms. Dealers often have an agreed price per product and a committed volume for the year. A customer agreement charges that price on the dealer's orders while it's active, and the portal shows how much of the commitment is ordered and how much is left.
  • Trade customers where several people touch one order. At a dealer, one person builds the cart and someone more senior signs it off. Contacts get a role (Buyer, Purchasing Manager, Finance or Viewer), and approval rules hold orders above an amount until the right role approves them in the portal.

Setting it up

  1. Install the module. It needs eCommerce, Sales and Invoicing, and adds a B2B menu under Website > Configuration.
  2. Open each trade customer's company contact and tick B2B Customer. Tick May Order on Account if they can confirm orders against payment terms instead of paying at checkout.
  3. On each contact person of that company, set the B2B Role: Buyer, Purchasing Manager, Finance or Viewer.
  4. On the product form, fill the Wholesale Quantities group: Minimum Quantity, Order Increment, Units per Case and, if needed, Maximum Quantity.
  5. If some customers should see only part of the range, create catalogues under Website > Configuration > B2B > Catalogues. While no catalogue applies to a customer, they see the whole shop.
  6. Add negotiated prices under B2B > Customer Agreements and order thresholds under B2B > Approval Rules. Then pick the Credit Manager in Website > Settings under B2B Accounts, so credit limit requests reach someone.

B2B Wholesale Portal: questions

Does it replace Odoo pricelists?

No. Pricelists still handle customer, group and quantity-break prices. The only price this module adds is the agreed price on an active customer agreement, which replaces the pricelist price on that customer's order lines until the agreement ends.

What happens when a buyer orders 25 and the case is 10?

Quantity rules are checked on the order line. In quick order, a quantity under the minimum is raised to it and a quantity off the increment is rounded up, with a note on the line saying why. Units per Case on its own is only shown to the buyer, so set an Order Increment if you want to force whole cases.

How does trade credit work?

It reads Odoo's own credit limit on the customer. The buyer's credit page shows the limit, unpaid invoices plus confirmed orders not yet invoiced, and what's available. A buyer can ask for a higher limit with a reason, and the credit manager approves it, which writes the new limit to the contact, or refuses it with a note the customer sees.

Can my sales reps place orders for their customers?

Yes. Users in the B2B Sales Rep group can switch, in the portal, to a B2B customer they're assigned to and order as that customer. The switch is logged on the customer's chatter and the quotation is marked as placed by the rep. There's also an Order for a Customer screen in the backend.

Can customers set up repeat orders?

A buyer can save the cart as an order template and apply it later, or reorder a past order. Templates can also run on a schedule, every week, two weeks, month or quarter, and a daily job places the due ones. Each time it re-checks today's catalogue and quantity rules and skips lines that are no longer allowed.

Are there AI features, and do they cost anything?

There's an optional B2B AI block in Website settings for natural-language catalogue search and an assistant for reps. It uses Anthropic's API with your own API key, so each use is billed by Anthropic. It's switched off by default and everything else works without it.
