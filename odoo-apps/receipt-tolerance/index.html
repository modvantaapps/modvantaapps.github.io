Receipt Tolerance

Stop over receipts and silent short deliveries: check every purchase receipt against the ordered quantity plus or minus a tolerance, then warn, ask a manager to approve, or block.

View on Odoo Apps Store Ask about customisation
Receipt Tolerance for Odoo 19

What it does

Odoo lets a warehouse clerk receive any quantity on a purchase receipt: 540 bolts on an order of 500 go into stock and onto the vendor bill without a question. This app sets an allowed tolerance for over-delivery and under-delivery, in percent or in units, for each product, vendor, product category or for the whole company. The tolerance is copied on every purchase order line. When a receipt is validated, the received total (earlier receipts included) is compared to the ordered quantity: inside the tolerance it goes through as usual, outside it the receipt is warned, sent to a tolerance manager for approval, or blocked - you choose, separately for too much and too little.

Who it is for

  • Distributors buying bulk items by weight or count. Vendors often ship a few percent more than ordered and the extra lands on the bill without anyone asking. A tolerance per product or vendor flags the receipt the moment it goes past the agreed limit.
  • Manufacturers receiving raw materials. Set one tolerance on a raw materials category and every sub-category uses it unless it has its own. Short deliveries inside the tolerance can close without leaving a backorder hanging around.
  • Companies where a manager signs off on exceptions. Instead of the clerk deciding, an over receipt can be sent for approval. Tolerance managers get an activity and approve or refuse on the receipt itself.
  • Buyers tracking which vendors deliver off-order. Every warning, request, approval, refusal and block is logged with the vendor, product and deviation, so you can see in a pivot who keeps shipping the wrong amount.

Setting it up

  1. Go to Purchase > Configuration > Settings and find the Receipt Tolerance block. Turn on Receipt Tolerance Check.
  2. Enter the default Over-delivery and Under-delivery values and choose % or Quantity for each.
  3. Under Outside Tolerance, set Too much received and Too little received to warn, require manager approval or block (under-delivery can also be Do not check).
  4. Under Backorders, decide on No Backorder Within Tolerance and set Shortage outside tolerance to Ask or Always create a backorder. Save.
  5. Where a product, vendor (Sales & Purchase tab) or product category needs its own rule, set its Receipt Tolerance field to Custom tolerance and fill the values, or No tolerance check.
  6. Add the users who should approve exceptions to the Tolerance Manager group. Inventory administrators already have it.
  7. Validate receipts as usual. Review results under Purchase > Reporting > Receipt Tolerance > Exceptions or By Order Line, and pending approvals under Inventory > Operations > Receipts to Approve.

Receipt Tolerance: questions

Which tolerance is used when several are set?

The order line takes its tolerance from the product first, then the vendor, then the product category and its parent categories, then the company default. Purchase managers can override it on a single line, and the Reset Receipt Tolerances action reads the rules again.

Does a second delivery count towards the limit?

Yes. The quantity already received on the purchase line is added to the receipt being validated, so a later delivery that pushes the total over the maximum is caught.

When does a short receipt count as under-delivery?

Only when the line is being closed, meaning the receipt is validated without a backorder and no other receipt is still open for that line. A partial delivery with a backorder isn't flagged.

What if quantities change after a manager approved them?

The approval is tied to the quantities that were approved. If the clerk edits them afterwards, the approval no longer matches and a new one is needed.

Are returns or receipts without a purchase order checked?

No. Only incoming moves from a vendor that are linked to a purchase order line are checked. Returns are skipped.

What does it need, and does it handle several companies?

It depends on Purchase and Inventory (purchase_stock), so Community is enough. Settings are per company and the exception log has a multi-company rule.
