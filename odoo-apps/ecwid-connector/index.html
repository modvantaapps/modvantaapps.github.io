Odoo Ecwid integration

Ecwid Connector

Connect your Ecwid store to Odoo - products, orders, customers, stock and coupons kept in sync

View on Odoo Apps Store Ask a question

What it does

Ecwid orders arrive as Odoo sales orders, products and their variations are linked by SKU, customers come in with their customer group, stock and prices go back to Ecwid, and webhooks trigger the right import as soon as something changes.

The connector links one or more Ecwid stores to Odoo through the Ecwid REST API and a custom app. Products are imported with their variations (combinations), categories, price and quantity and linked to Odoo products by SKU; you can also export Odoo products to Ecwid and push stock and prices.

Ecwid orders become Odoo sales orders with the customer, lines, shipping and discounts, and refunds are imported too. Customers are imported with their customer group, and discount coupons are listed with their type, status, limits and how many orders used them. Order status, fulfilment and cancellations can be written back to Ecwid.

Who it is for

  • Ecwid merchants who keep their books in Odoo. A small brand selling through an Ecwid storefront can leave the shop where it is and have each order arrive in Odoo as a sales order, with taxes mapped to Odoo taxes, shipping lines and discounts, ready for delivery and invoicing.
  • Sellers with wholesale and retail buyers. Ecwid customer groups can each be tied to an Odoo pricelist and a contact tag, and wholesale price tiers can come in as a pricelist, so trade customers keep their own prices on both sides.
  • Shops that sell on more than one channel. When Odoo holds the stock for a market stall, a marketplace and the Ecwid shop, it can publish on hand, free or forecasted quantity to Ecwid, either by setting the number or by sending each stock move as an adjustment.
  • Businesses running more than one Ecwid store. Each store is its own record with its own token, warehouse, pricelist and mappings, so two shops in one company don't mix their products or orders.

Setting it up

  1. Install the module and open Ecwid > Configuration > Stores. A demo store with sample data answers every call locally if you want to try the screens first.
  2. In your Ecwid admin, open #develop-apps, create a custom app, and copy the store ID, the secret token and the client secret.
  3. Use Ecwid > Configuration > Connect a Store, or create a store record, paste the Store ID, Secret Token and Client Secret on the Connection tab, then click Test Connection.
  4. On the Products tab choose Match Existing Products (SKU, barcode or both) and what to update on import. On the Stock tab pick Stock to Publish and the Stock Export Mode.
  5. On Orders & Payments set the tax handling, fulfilment mode and which statuses go back to Ecwid. On Mappings link shipping methods, taxes, customer groups and payment methods.
  6. On the Webhooks tab copy the webhook URL into your Ecwid app's webhookUrl and pick the order, product, customer and category events.
  7. Click Import Products, then Import Orders, and watch Ecwid > Monitoring > Queue and Sync Log for anything that needs attention.

Ecwid Connector: questions

What do I need on the Ecwid side?

A custom app created in your Ecwid admin under #develop-apps, which gives you the store ID, a secret token for API calls and a client secret used to verify webhooks. The connector talks to the Ecwid REST API v3.

Can I try it without an Ecwid account?

Yes. Demo mode uses a built-in sample store and never calls the network, so you can import products, orders, a refund and coupons to see how everything looks. There is also a test API URL option for developers pointing at a staging endpoint.

How are refunds and cancellations handled?

Refunds made in Ecwid can be imported as credit notes, and posted automatically if you choose. In the other direction, cancelling in Odoo can be pushed to Ecwid, and credit notes can be sent back as a refund status.

What if a webhook is missed or fails?

Webhook calls are checked against the X-Ecwid-Webhook-Signature header, logged and queued, and repeated events are recognised as duplicates. Scheduled imports keep running as a fallback, the queue retries failed jobs, and a daily digest reports failures.

Does it need Enterprise or the CRM app?

No. It depends on Sales, Inventory with delivery methods and Invoicing, all in Community. Creating CRM opportunities from orders is optional and only works if the CRM app is installed.

Who ships the orders?

You choose per store. If Odoo ships, the fulfilment status and tracking number are pushed back to Ecwid when the delivery is validated. If Ecwid ships, its fulfilment status validates the Odoo delivery instead.
