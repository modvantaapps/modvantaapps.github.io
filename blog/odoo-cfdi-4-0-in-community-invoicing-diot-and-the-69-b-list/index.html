Odoo CFDI 4.0 in Community: Invoicing, DIOT and the 69-B List

How to handle Odoo CFDI 4.0 invoicing, the DIOT and SAT 69-B list checks in Odoo Community, with the fields, steps and mistakes we see most often.

Picture a distributor in Mexico that moved its sales, purchases and stock into Odoo Community last year. The warehouse is happy and the sales team is happy. Finance is not. Every invoice still gets retyped into a PAC web portal so it can be stamped. Every month someone exports vendor bills to a spreadsheet to build the DIOT. And once in a while, somebody remembers to check suppliers against the SAT 69-B list, usually after a scare.

We see this setup a lot. It works, more or less, until the volume grows or the person who knows the spreadsheet goes on holiday. This post covers the three Mexican compliance jobs that Odoo Community doesn't do out of the box, and how we approach each one.

Why Community is different from Enterprise here

Odoo Community ships the Mexican localization basics: a chart of accounts, taxes and fiscal positions. What it doesn't include is electronic invoicing. The CFDI connector lives in Enterprise. So if you're on Community, you either pay for Enterprise licences, keep a parallel process outside Odoo, or add modules that fill the gap.

There's no single right answer. If you already want other Enterprise apps, the licence may make sense. If Community does everything else you need, adding a few focused modules is usually cheaper and keeps your data in one place. That's the path this post assumes.

CFDI 4.0: what actually goes wrong

CFDI 4.0 tightened the rules on the receiver side. The invoice now has to carry the customer's RFC, their name exactly as it appears on their Constancia de Situación Fiscal, their tax regime and the postal code of their fiscal address. If any of those don't match what the SAT has on file, the PAC rejects the invoice.

In practice, almost every rejection we see comes from customer data, not from the invoice itself. A few typical cases:

  • The customer name in Odoo includes "S.A. de C.V." and the SAT record doesn't, or the other way round. For legal entities, the regime suffix shouldn't go in the name field.
  • The postal code on the contact is the delivery address, not the fiscal address.
  • Someone created the contact with the RFC in the wrong field, or with spaces in it.
  • The tax regime was never filled in because nobody knew it was needed.

So before any module goes live, we clean the contacts. Open Contacts, filter by country Mexico, and go through the ones you invoice regularly. Ask each customer for a current Constancia de Situación Fiscal and copy the details across. It's boring work. It also saves you weeks of rejected invoices.

The second source of trouble is product and unit codes. Each invoice line needs a SAT product/service key and a SAT unit key. If your catalogue has two thousand products, set the keys at the product category level where you can and override only the exceptions. Doing it product by product is how people burn out in week one.

Then there's the "uso CFDI" and payment method on each invoice. We usually set sensible defaults per customer so the salesperson doesn't have to think about it every time. Payment form (forma de pago) and method (PUE or PPD) matter more than people expect, because PPD invoices need payment complements later.

How our CFDI module fits in

Our Odoo Mexico CFDI 4.0 module issues CFDI 4.0 invoices and credit notes from Odoo. The XML is checked against the official SAT schema before it leaves, and it's sealed inside your own database with your CSD. That last point matters to a lot of finance managers: your private key stays in your Odoo, not on some third-party server you don't control.

Checking against the schema before stamping catches structural errors early, so you see the problem on the invoice form instead of reading a cryptic rejection code from the PAC. It won't catch a customer name that doesn't match the SAT record, though. Only clean data does that.

A practical tip: keep your CSD certificate's expiry date somewhere visible. Certificates expire, and when one does, invoicing stops. We've seen it catch companies out on the last working day of the month more than once.

The DIOT without the spreadsheet

The DIOT (Declaración Informativa de Operaciones con Terceros) reports your operations with suppliers, broken down by supplier and by tax treatment. Most companies build it from a vendor bill export, a pivot table and a lot of patience.

The SAT updated the layout for 2025, and the carga masiva TXT file now follows a 54 field structure. If your old spreadsheet macro was built for the previous layout, it needs rework anyway. That makes this a good moment to move the job into Odoo.

Our Mexico DIOT 2025 module generates the carga masiva TXT from your Odoo accounting using that 54 field layout. The file is only as good as the data behind it, so here's what we check first:

  1. Supplier type and nationality. Domestic suppliers, foreign suppliers and "global" suppliers are reported differently. Make sure each vendor contact says which one it is.
  2. Foreign supplier details. Foreign vendors don't have a Mexican RFC, so their tax ID and country need to be filled in properly on the contact.
  3. Tax mapping. Every tax you use on vendor bills has to land in the right DIOT column. If someone created a custom "IVA 16% servicios" tax two years ago, it needs to be mapped too, or those amounts quietly go missing.
  4. Payment timing. Since IVA is generally reported on a cash basis, what counts is when the bill was paid, not when it was posted. Bills paid outside Odoo or reconciled late will skew the report.

Run the file for a past month you've already filed, and compare it line by line with what you submitted. If the numbers match, you can trust it going forward. If they don't, the differences will point straight at the data problems.

Rules and layouts change. Before you file, confirm the current requirements with your accountant or on the SAT portal.

The 69-B list: checking suppliers before it hurts

The SAT publishes a list of taxpayers presumed or confirmed to have issued invoices for operations that didn't really happen. People call it the lista negra, EFOS 69-B, or just "the 69-B list". If one of your suppliers ends up on it, the tax effect of the invoices you received from them can be challenged, and you may have to prove the operations were real or correct your position.

The list is updated regularly. Checking it once when you onboard a supplier isn't enough, because a supplier who was clean last year might not be clean now.

What we usually see is a manual check: someone downloads the CSV from the SAT site and searches for RFCs one at a time. It's slow, and it gets skipped when things are busy.

Our EFOS 69-B module checks the SAT 69-B list against every RFC in your Odoo. That covers your whole contact base, not just the vendors someone remembered to look up. When a match turns up, the useful next step is usually:

  • Pause new purchase orders to that supplier until someone in finance reviews it.
  • Pull the bills you've received from them and look at the dates against the listing.
  • Talk to your accountant about what documentation you have and what you should do.

We'd also suggest checking customers, not just suppliers. It's less common to need it, but it's useful to know who you're dealing with.

Questions we get asked

Can we keep using our current PAC?

Usually, yes. Most companies already have a PAC contract and stamping credits, and there's no reason to throw that away. During setup we confirm how your PAC fits with the module and test stamping in a sandbox before anything goes live.

Do these modules work on older Odoo versions?

The app pages list Odoo 19. If you're on an older version, get in touch and we'll tell you honestly what's involved. Sometimes it's a small adaptation. Sometimes upgrading first makes more sense, and we handle migrations too through our services.

How long does it take to get running?

That depends far more on your data than on the software. A company with clean contacts and a tidy product catalogue can move quickly. One with years of messy contacts will spend most of the time on cleanup. We'd rather tell you that up front than pretend otherwise.
