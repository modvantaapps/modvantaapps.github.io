Odoo SII DTE and Boleta Electrónica in POS: A Practical Guide

How Odoo SII DTE and boleta electrónica work together in Chile: CAF folios, POS setup, sending to the SII and the mistakes we see most in live stores.

Picture a hardware store with two counters and a small B2B side business. Over the counter they sell screws, paint and tools to walk-in customers. Through the back office they invoice contractors who buy on credit. On a Saturday morning the queue is long, the POS is printing tickets, and a contractor walks up and says "factura, por favor". The cashier has no idea how to switch from boleta to factura in the system, so she writes the RUT on a piece of paper and promises to send the invoice on Monday.

We see versions of this all the time. The accounting side of Odoo is set up for SII electronic documents, but the point of sale lives in its own world, and the two don't quite meet. This post is about making them meet.

Two document families, one SII

When people search for "Odoo SII DTE boleta", they're usually mixing two related but different things.

DTE (Documento Tributario Electrónico) is the umbrella. It covers the documents a finance team normally issues from the back office: the factura electrónica (type 33), factura exenta (34), notas de crédito (61) and débito (56), and the guía de despacho (52). These are issued to a business with a RUT, they go through the SII's DTE submission service, and the receiver can accept or reject them.

The boleta electrónica (type 39, and boleta exenta, type 41) is the retail document. It's what a final consumer gets at the counter. It's still a DTE in the broad sense, but in practice the SII handles boletas through a separate submission channel, and the volume is completely different. A distributor might issue forty facturas a day. A busy shop issues hundreds of boletas.

That difference in volume is why we always treat them as two pieces of work, even when the client wants one project. In our setups, the back office DTE flow runs through the Chile SII DTE module for Odoo 19, and the counter flow runs through the Chile Boleta Electrónica POS module, which issues boleta 39 and boleta exenta 41 directly from the Odoo Point of Sale. Same company, same certificate, same SII, but different screens and different people using them.

What has to be in place before anything works

Most of the delays we see on Chilean projects have nothing to do with code. They come from missing paperwork. Before we touch Odoo, we ask for these:

  1. The digital certificate (certificado digital) of the person authorised to sign documents before the SII, as a .pfx or .p12 file with its password. Check the expiry date. A certificate that expires in three weeks will stop your invoicing in three weeks.
  2. CAF files (Código de Autorización de Folios) for every document type you plan to issue. You download these from the SII site. One CAF for facturas, one for boletas, one for notas de crédito, and so on. A CAF covers a range of folio numbers, and when the range runs out, you stop issuing that document type.
  3. The company data exactly as the SII has it: RUT, razón social, giro, actividad económica codes, address and comuna. If the giro in Odoo doesn't match what the SII has on file, you'll get rejections that look mysterious until you compare the two side by side.
  4. Confirmation of where you are in the SII process: whether the company is already authorised as an electronic issuer, or still has to go through the certification steps in the SII test environment.

That last point catches people out. Moving from a different software provider to Odoo is a different exercise from becoming an electronic issuer for the first time. The SII's requirements here have changed over the years, so confirm your situation with your accountant or directly with the SII before you plan dates.

Setting up the back office side

On the accounting side, the work mostly happens in three places.

Under Settings > Companies, the company record holds the RUT, giro and activity codes. Under Accounting > Configuration > Journals, the sales journal is where the Chilean document types are enabled and where you decide which documents get issued from which journal. Then the CAF files get loaded per document type, and the certificate gets attached to the company.

Once that's done, the daily flow is plain. A user validates an invoice in Accounting > Customers > Invoices, Odoo assigns the next folio from the CAF range, signs the XML, sends it, and stores the track ID the SII returns. The invoice then moves through statuses until the SII accepts or rejects it.

What usually goes wrong:

  • Customers without a proper RUT or giro. Clean the contact data before go-live. A contact imported from an old system with "RUT: 11.111.111-1" as a placeholder will bite you.
  • Credit notes without a reference. A nota de crédito has to point to the document it corrects. If someone creates a credit note from scratch instead of using the reverse button on the original invoice, the reference is missing and the document fails.
  • Nobody watching rejected documents. Make one person responsible for checking SII statuses every day. A rejected factura sitting unnoticed for two weeks is a real problem with a customer who wants to claim their tax credit.

Bringing the boleta to the counter

The POS side is where the hardware store story gets fixed.

In Point of Sale > Configuration > Settings, each POS is tied to its journal and its document behaviour. The goal is that the default ticket is a boleta (39, or 41 for exempt sales), and that the cashier can switch to a factura when a business customer asks for one, by picking or creating the customer with RUT and giro right there on the screen.

A few things we pay attention to in every POS rollout:

The printed ticket. A boleta electrónica carries the timbre electrónico, the barcode that lets anyone verify the document. That barcode has to print legibly on a thermal printer, which usually means 80 mm paper. We've seen shops buy 58 mm printers to save money and then fight with the layout for weeks. Test with your actual printer before go-live, not a PDF on screen.

Folio supply. Boletas burn through folios fast. Request a CAF range that matches your volume and set a reminder well before the range ends. Running out of folios on a Friday afternoon is a story we hear too often, and it's completely avoidable.

Internet drops. Retail connections fail. Think about what the cashier should do when the line is down: keep selling and send later, or switch to a backup process. Agree on this with the store manager and write it down, because the cashier will not improvise well with a queue in front of them.

Returns. A customer comes back with a boleta and wants a refund. That's a nota de crédito referencing the original boleta. Train the cashiers to start the refund from the original order in the POS, so the reference is carried over, rather than ringing up a negative sale.

Closing the session. The POS session closing in Odoo is where the cashier counts the drawer and the sales post to accounting. If sessions stay open for days, the accounting team can't reconcile. Make closing the session part of the shift routine.

Testing before you go live

We always run the whole flow in the SII test environment first. Issue facturas, issue boletas from the POS, issue a credit note against each, and print every one of them on the real printers. Then do it again with the people who will actually use the system, not just with the project team.

The cashier test is the one that finds the real problems. Developers know where the factura switch is. A cashier on her second day doesn't.

Questions we get asked

Can the same Odoo database issue both facturas and boletas?

Yes. One company, one certificate, separate CAF files per document type. Facturas usually come from the accounting side, boletas from the POS, and a POS can also issue a factura when the customer gives a RUT.

Do we need to change anything when the SII changes its rules?

Sometimes. The SII adjusts formats, submission channels and requirements from time to time. When that happens, the module may need an update. This is a good reason to have a support arrangement in place rather than treating e-invoicing as a one-off install. Check our services page for how we handle ongoing support.

We already use another POS. Can we keep it and just use Odoo for invoicing?

You can, but then you need an integration between the two, and boletas from the other system still have to be issued and reported correctly. In most retail setups we find it simpler to move the counter into the Odoo POS, so stock, sales and tax documents live in one place.
