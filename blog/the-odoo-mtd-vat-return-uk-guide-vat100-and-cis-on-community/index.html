The Odoo MTD VAT Return UK Guide: VAT100 and CIS on Community

How to file an Odoo MTD VAT return in the UK from Community: build the VAT100 boxes, keep an audit trail, and handle CIS deductions for construction.

Picture a building contractor with a dozen regular subcontractors, running Odoo Community for quotes, purchase orders and invoicing. Everything lands in Odoo. Then the VAT quarter closes, and the finance manager exports a tax report, opens a spreadsheet, retypes nine numbers into a bridging tool, and hopes nothing moved since last month. The CIS deductions live in a second spreadsheet that one person understands and nobody else can check.

That's the most common story we see on UK Odoo Community databases. It works, right up until someone asks "where did box 7 come from?" and the answer is a copy-paste chain nobody can reproduce.

Here's what's actually going on under the hood, and how we usually fix it.

What Community gives you, and what it doesn't

Install the UK fiscal localization (l10n_uk) and you get a UK chart of accounts and a set of UK taxes, and those taxes carry tax grids. The grids are the important bit: each tax line is tagged so the system knows the net belongs in one box and the VAT in another.

What Community does not ship is the renderer that turns those grids into a VAT100. The dynamic accounting reports, including the UK VAT return layout and the HMRC connection, sit in Enterprise. In Community you get a plain tax report listing taxes with net and tax amounts, which is useful for checking but is not a VAT return and is definitely not a digital record you can hand an inspector.

So you have three honest options. Move to Enterprise. Keep retyping into a bridging tool and accept the manual step. Or add a module that computes the return inside Odoo from the same move lines the accounts are built on.

That third route is what our UK VAT100 Return and VAT Audit app for Odoo Community does: it computes the boxes in Odoo 19 Community and gives you the backing detail behind each one. How the figures then reach HMRC is a separate decision, and one to make with your accountant, because MTD requires the submission to go through a recognised route and expects digital links rather than retyping between systems.

Our opinion, after doing this a few times: the submission mechanism matters less than the audit trail. If you can click box 4 and see the twelve bills that make it up, you will survive a check. If you can't, the submission being automated won't save you.

Getting the nine boxes to come out right

Most wrong returns we see are not caused by the report. They're caused by taxes that were created by hand and never tagged.

Someone needed a zero-rate for a new product line, duplicated an existing tax, changed the rate, and saved. The tax calculates fine on invoices. But open it in Accounting > Configuration > Taxes, look at the Definition tab, and the distribution lines have no tax grid. That tax is now invisible to the return. The net never reaches box 6 and nobody notices for a year.

The checks we run on every UK database:

  1. Every active tax has grids on both the invoice and the credit note distribution lines.
  2. Reverse charge taxes are built as a pair of 100% and -100% lines so the VAT lands in box 1 and box 4 and the net goes where it belongs.
  3. Postponed VAT Accounting on imports is modelled as a real tax, not a manual journal entry someone remembers to post.
  4. Manual journal entries that carry VAT have the tax grid set on the journal item, because Odoo will not guess it for you.
  5. The tax return lock date in Accounting > Configuration > Settings is set after each filing, so a backdated bill can't quietly change a period you've already submitted.

That last one causes more pain than anything else. Without a lock date, somebody posts a supplier bill into a closed quarter, the recomputed return no longer matches what you filed, and you spend an afternoon working out which number was right.

For construction, the domestic reverse charge for building and construction services deserves its own test invoices. The subcontractor invoices without VAT, the contractor accounts for it on both sides, and the net value behaves differently in box 6 and box 7 depending on which side of the transaction you're on. Set up one sale and one purchase with the reverse charge tax, post them, run the return, and read the boxes before you trust the configuration on real volume. Rules here change and the detail is fiddly, so confirm your treatment with your accountant or with HMRC rather than with a forum post from three versions ago.

CIS is where the VAT return quietly breaks

The single most common CIS mistake in Odoo: deducting the scheme amount by editing the bill.

A subcontractor bills labour plus VAT. Someone reduces the labour line, or adds a negative line, so the bill total matches what will actually be paid. The payment reconciles, everyone's happy. Except the deduction is not a discount. It's money withheld from the subcontractor and owed to HMRC, and the VAT is still calculated on the full value. Shrink the line and you under-report box 7, understate the subcontractor's VAT, and lose the figure you need for the monthly CIS return.

The correct shape is: the bill stays at full value with VAT on the full value, the deduction is recorded separately against a CIS liability account, and the payment is the difference. Materials are excluded from the calculation, so the bill has to distinguish labour from materials rather than carrying one lump-sum line. Our UK CIS app for Odoo 19 applies the deduction on the vendor bill at the gross, net or higher rate and records the liability, so the month-end figure comes out of the same ledger as everything else.

Two practical habits that save time:

  • Verify subcontractors before the first payment and store the result against the partner record, because the rate you apply depends on it. Guessing is expensive.
  • Keep the CIS liability account separate from PAYE. When they share an account, reconciling what you owe becomes arithmetic rather than a balance you can read.

Worth asking early whether a subcontractor's invoices fall under the reverse charge as well as CIS, because plenty do, and the two interact. Get both wrong at once and the clean-up is a line-by-line reposting exercise.

Questions we get asked

Do we have to move to Enterprise to comply with MTD?

No. MTD is about keeping digital records and submitting through a recognised route, not about which Odoo edition you own. Community can hold the records and compute the return. What Community lacks out of the box is the UK return layout, and that gap can be filled with a module.

Can one setup cover a UK company and a subsidiary abroad?

Yes, with separate companies in the same database and the right localization on each. The thing to decide up front is the chart of accounts, because changing it after you've posted a year of entries is a project, not a setting. We cover that kind of multi-country work under Odoo localization.

Our VAT control account never matches the return. Where do we start?

Start with manual journal entries in the period, then unreconciled payments on tax-bearing invoices, then taxes missing grids. In our experience it's almost always one of those three, and it's usually the first.
