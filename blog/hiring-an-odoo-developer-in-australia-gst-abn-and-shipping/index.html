Hiring an Odoo Developer in Australia: GST, ABN and Shipping

A practical guide to hiring an Odoo developer in Australia: GST handling, ABN validation, Australia Post shipping and working with an offshore team.

Picture a wholesaler running a few hundred orders a month out of one warehouse. Invoicing sits in one system, stock sits in another, and the shipping labels are typed by hand into a carrier portal every afternoon. They move the lot into Odoo over a long weekend, everyone is pleased, and then the first BAS period rolls around and the numbers don't line up with what the accountant expects.

That's the pattern we see most often. Odoo itself is rarely the problem. The problem is that the Australian parts of the setup got treated as an afternoon job: tax codes, ABNs, freight. Those three things are where an Australian Odoo build either holds up or quietly rots for a year.

So here's what we'd actually check, in the order we'd check it.

Get the tax setup right before you customise anything

First question we ask on any Australian project: was the Australian fiscal localisation package installed when the database was created, or did someone build taxes by hand? Go to Settings, turn on developer tools if you like, and look at Accounting, then Configuration, then Taxes. If you see one tax called "GST" and nothing else, someone built it by hand and you will have trouble at reporting time.

The localisation gives you a chart of accounts and a set of taxes mapped to the BAS labels, plus the tax report under Accounting, then Reporting, then Tax Report. The mapping is the valuable bit. Each tax carries tax grids, and those grids are what make the report add up. A hand-made tax with the right percentage and no grids will post to the general ledger perfectly well and show up nowhere on your BAS.

Three settings cause more trouble than everything else combined:

  • Rounding method. Accounting settings let you round tax per line or globally. Change it after a year of invoices and your totals will shift by cents against documents you've already sent. Pick it at the start, with your accountant, and leave it.
  • Tax-included pricing. B2C sellers usually want prices shown with GST in them. That's a flag on the tax plus the pricelist setup, and it has to be consistent across the website, the point of sale and the sales order. Half-inclusive, half-exclusive is the single most common cause of "the website total doesn't match the invoice".
  • Fiscal positions. Customers overseas, customers in different registration situations, and GST free product lines all belong in fiscal positions on the customer record, not in someone's head. Set the fiscal position on the contact and Odoo swaps the tax automatically on every new order.

Also check that your own ABN prints on the invoice PDF. In Odoo the company tax ID field holds it, and the external report layout needs to show it. Tax invoices have content requirements, and those requirements change from time to time, so confirm the current wording with your accountant or directly with the ATO rather than trusting whatever the last developer put in the template.

ABNs: validate them where the data lives

Most companies we talk to keep ABNs in a text field, or in a spreadsheet a bookkeeper maintains. Then a supplier changes their registration status, nobody notices, and you find out when someone is reconciling a quarter later.

Validating an ABN is cheap to automate and nobody wants to do it by hand. The check digit alone catches typos, and the register will also tell you the entity name, the ACN, and whether the business is currently registered for GST. That last field matters for how you treat the bill in front of you, and no-ABN withholding is a real thing your accountant will want to talk about.

We built a module for this because we kept doing the same work twice: it validates the Australian Business Number on the contact form, pulls the ACN and the GST registration status, and keeps the result on the partner record where the rest of your data lives. The rest of our published apps sit in /odoo-apps/ if you want to see what we maintain.

The habit we'd push for: validate at the point of entry. If a salesperson creates a contact with a bad ABN on Tuesday, you want the complaint on Tuesday, not at year end.

Australia Post without the surprises

Freight is where custom work usually earns its money. Australia is a long way from everywhere, including itself, so shipping cost is a real line in your margin rather than a rounding error.

A few things that go wrong, in rough order of frequency.

  1. Product weights and dimensions are blank. Odoo cannot ask a carrier for a rate on a parcel with no weight. You'll either get zero freight or an error at checkout. Fill in the weight and volume on the product's Inventory tab, and set up packaging records for your real cartons and satchels instead of letting Odoo guess.
  2. Address data isn't clean enough for a label. Suburb, state and postcode have to agree. We'd rather validate the address on the sales order than hand a bad label to a driver.
  3. Business rules the carrier doesn't know about. Authority to leave, parcel lockers, PO boxes you can't deliver to, split shipments when one line is on backorder. These are custom fields and small workflow changes on the delivery order, and they're exactly the kind of thing custom module work exists for.

If live rate quoting is more than you need right now, Odoo's rule-based delivery methods will take you a long way. Under Inventory, then Configuration, then Delivery Methods, you can build a pricing table on weight, order value or destination, and charge a flat rate per zone. It's less impressive than a live API call and it's honest about what you actually pay. Plenty of businesses run this way for years.

Working with an offshore team on Australian hours

We're remote, and we'll be straight about the trade-off. The time difference between India and the Australian east coast is a few hours, which means a working overlap most mornings for you and most afternoons for us. That's enough for a daily call and quick decisions. It is not enough for someone to sit next to your warehouse manager all day, so the way you run the engagement matters more than it would locally.

What we insist on:

  • A staging database that matches production, including the Odoo version and your custom modules. No testing on live.
  • Changes in modules under version control, with real code, not clicked together in Studio where nobody can review it or move it between databases.
  • Test data that looks Australian: real postcodes, real ABNs, GST free lines, a credit note.
  • A named person on your side who can answer process questions. One person, not a committee.
  • Written handover for anything custom, so you are not locked to us. If you want to change hands later, you should be able to.

And agree up front on what happens after go-live, because something always comes up in the first month. We keep that separate as ongoing support: bug fixing, security review, performance checks, small improvements. Questions we get asked

Can you work on our existing Odoo database, or do you need to start again?

We can work on an existing one, and we usually do. The first thing we'd do is look at how the taxes and the chart of accounts were set up, because if the localisation was never installed, that's a conversation about fixing the foundation before adding anything on top.

Do we need Odoo Enterprise for Australian tax reporting?

Not necessarily, and it depends which reports and which version you're on. Community handles a lot. We'd rather look at your actual requirements than sell you a licence tier. Your accountant should be in that conversation too.

How do you handle GST questions you're not sure about?

We tell you we're not sure. We're developers, not tax agents, and Australian rules change. What we do is build the system so your accountant's answer can be configured rather than hard-coded, then test it against documents they've already signed off.
