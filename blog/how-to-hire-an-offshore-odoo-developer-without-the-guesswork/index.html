How to Hire an Offshore Odoo Developer Without the Guesswork

A practical guide to hire an Odoo developer offshore: how to write the brief, compare pricing models, agree on scope and manage the work remotely.

Most people searching for an offshore Odoo developer have the same experience. You write a short description of what you need, send it to five firms, and get back five answers that have nothing in common. One quotes a few hundred dollars. Another quotes twenty thousand. A third replies with a questionnaire you are not sure how to fill in. Nothing in those replies tells you who will actually finish the job.

The usual cause is not the developer's skill. It is that the request was too vague to price, so each vendor quietly guessed at a different project. Fix the brief and most of the confusion disappears. This guide covers the three things that decide whether a remote Odoo engagement works: how you describe the work, how you pay for it, and how you manage it once the contract is signed.

Write a brief a developer can price

A good brief does not need technical language. It needs facts. Before you contact anyone, write down the following:

  • Your Odoo version and edition. For example Odoo 18 Community or Odoo 17 Enterprise. Behaviour and upgrade paths differ between versions, so this changes the estimate.
  • Where it runs. Odoo Online, Odoo.sh, or your own server. Odoo Online does not allow custom code, which rules out many requests immediately.
  • Which apps you already use. Sales, Inventory, Accounting, Manufacturing, Subscriptions, and so on.
  • The process you want to change, written as numbered steps a new employee could follow.
  • What "finished" looks like. A sentence such as "a delivery driver can mark twenty stops as delivered on a phone, offline, and the invoice updates when they reconnect."
  • Rough numbers. Users, orders per day, products, years of history. Volume affects design more than most buyers expect.
  • Systems that must connect. Name them: a specific payment provider, courier, marketplace, or bank feed.
  • Dates that cannot move, such as a financial year end or a warehouse opening.

Describe the process, not the screen

Many briefs arrive as a list of fields and buttons. That hides the real requirement and usually produces something nobody wants to use. Describe the work instead: who does what, in which order, what goes wrong today, and who needs to approve each step. A developer who understands the process will often propose a shorter path using standard Odoo features, which saves you money. This is the difference between a prebuilt module from a catalogue like our Odoo apps and genuinely custom Odoo development built around your workflow.

It also helps to say what is normal in your sector. Stock handling in food distribution is not stock handling in electronics. If a vendor has already worked in your industry, say so in the brief and ask them what surprised them last time.

Pick a pricing model that fits the work

There are three common ways to engage a remote Odoo developer, and each suits a different kind of problem. We offer all three on our hire an Odoo developer page, and the choice matters more than the hourly figure.

Fixed price works when the scope is clear and stable. A migration, a defined integration, or a module with written acceptance criteria. You know the cost before you start. The trade-off is that changes mid-project need a variation, because the price was built on the original scope.

Hour blocks work when you have a queue of small jobs: report tweaks, access rights, a stubborn automation, a quarterly clean-up. You buy a block of hours and draw them down. This avoids a separate quote for every half-day task, but you need to track what the hours were spent on.

A dedicated developer works when you have months of continuous work and want someone who learns your business. The cost is predictable per month. It only pays off if you can keep the person busy and someone on your side is available to answer questions.

A warning on hourly rates. A low rate with poor communication costs more than a higher rate with clear weekly output, because you pay twice for the same misunderstanding. Compare total delivered scope, not the rate card.

Questions to ask before you sign

  1. Which Odoo versions do you currently work on, and have you done this type of work on my version?
  2. Will the code be written as a proper Odoo module, or as changes to core files? Only the first is upgradeable.
  3. Who owns the code when the project ends, and will it be delivered to a repository I control?
  4. Will you work on a staging database, and how do you move changes to live?
  5. How will you handle my data during development? Can you work with anonymised or sample data?
  6. What is included in testing, and what counts as a bug versus a change request?
  7. What happens after go-live? Is there a warranty period for defects, and what does support cost?
  8. Who will I actually speak to each week, in which time zone, and in which language?
  9. How do you handle scope changes: a written variation, or a verbal agreement?
  10. Can you describe a project that went wrong and what you changed afterwards?

The last question is the most revealing. A straight answer tells you more than a portfolio.

Contracts, invoicing and tax

Cross-border software work brings a few administrative points that are easy to settle early and painful to settle late. Treat the following as things to raise with your accountant, not as advice:

  • Contract basics. Scope, payment schedule, intellectual property ownership, confidentiality, and what happens if either side stops. Keep it short but written.
  • Invoicing and currency. Agree the currency, who absorbs bank charges, and the payment terms in days.
  • Indirect tax. Many countries apply a reverse-charge or self-assessment mechanism when a business imports services. Your accountant will tell you how to record it.
  • Withholding tax. Some countries require the buyer to withhold tax on payments to foreign suppliers, sometimes reduced by a double taxation treaty.
  • Contractor status. If you engage one person full time for a long period and direct their daily work, some jurisdictions may treat that as employment rather than a supplier relationship.

These rules differ by country and change over time. Confirm the current position with a local accountant or your tax authority before you set up the arrangement.

Manage the work in short cycles

Offshore projects rarely fail in one dramatic moment. They drift. Short cycles stop the drift.

  • Ask for a working demo every week or two on a staging database you can log into yourself.
  • Give feedback in writing against the brief, not in long calls.
  • Test with real-looking data, including the awkward cases: refunds, part-deliveries, cancelled orders.
  • Nominate one person on your side to answer questions. Unanswered questions are the single biggest cause of delay.
  • Keep a shared list of open items with owners and dates.
  • Ask for brief handover notes and a short video walkthrough before final payment.
  • Review access rights and remove developer accounts from live once the work is accepted.

Watch for early warning signs: no demo in three weeks, answers that avoid your question, work starting before the scope is written down, or a single developer who will not let you see the staging environment.

Frequently asked questions

Can I hire an offshore Odoo developer for a small job?

Yes. Small, well-defined tasks are often the easiest to outsource because the scope is obvious. Hour blocks usually suit this better than a fixed-price contract, since the overhead of quoting each task separately is high.

How do I protect my business data?

Ask to work on a staging database with anonymised or sample data where possible, sign a confidentiality clause, give access only to the apps the developer needs, and remove accounts when the work ends.

What if the developer disappears halfway through?

This is why code ownership and repository access belong in the contract from day one. If the module sits in a repository you control and is written as a standard Odoo module, another developer can pick it up.
